# The walk, milestone 1: Implementation Plan

> **For Claude:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task.

**Goal:** `/walk/`, a scroll-driven walk through a stand-in point-cloud alley
with three stops (About, CV, Contact), good enough to judge pacing and look
before real scans are chosen.

**Architecture:** A standalone Eleventy page with its own Vite entry
(`walk.js`, `walk.css`), so the main site bundle is untouched. A pure
timeline module maps scroll progress to a position on a Catmull-Rom path that
dwells at stops; a stand-in generator yields point arrays through the same
shape the real scan loader will; three.js draws the points with a custom
shader; screens are DOM elements placed with `CSS3DRenderer`.

**Tech Stack:** Eleventy 3, Vite 7, three.js 0.186 (`three`, `three/addons`),
node:test, Playwright for the visual check.

Design: `docs/plans/2026-10-03-walk-design.md`.

### Task 2: Timeline (pure, tested)

**Files:**
- Create: `src/js/walk/timeline.js`
- Test: `tests/walk-timeline.test.js`

`createTimeline(stops, { dwell })` where each stop has `at` (0..1 along the
path). Scroll progress is split into travel segments and dwell segments of
equal weight `dwell`; inside a dwell the path position holds at the stop and
`focus` rises 0 to 1 to 0 so the camera can turn to the screen.
`timeline.at(progress) -> { t, stop, focus }` and
`timeline.progressOf(index)` (the middle of stop `index`'s dwell, for key
stepping).

Tests: endpoints map to t=0 and t=1; t never decreases as progress rises;
across the middle half of a dwell t is the stop's `at` and focus is above 0.9;
in travel focus is 0; `progressOf(i)` lands on stop i with focus 1.

### Task 3: Stand-in scene (pure, tested)

**Files:**
- Create: `src/js/walk/standin.js`
- Test: `tests/walk-standin.test.js`

`standinAlley({ seed, length, density, figures })` returns
`{ positions: Float32Array, colors: Uint8Array, count }`: floor, two uneven
walls, a few lamps that warm nearby points, overhead wires, and a seated or
standing figure (capsules: body, head, arms toward a held object) at each
figure anchor. Seeded so it is stable between loads. The real loader returns
the same shape from a binary file.

Tests: same seed gives identical arrays; count matches array lengths; all
points within the alley bounds; each figure anchor has points within 1 m.

### Task 4: Points renderer

**Files:**
- Create: `src/js/walk/points.js`

`THREE.Points` with a `ShaderMaterial`: size attenuated by distance, round
soft sprite in the fragment shader, fade to black with distance (fog),
low-frequency breathing offset from a hash of the vertex index and time,
points within ~1.5 m of the camera pushed outward (scatter), colour
desaturated toward a single tone. Additive-free, depth-tested, so it reads as
dust, not glow. Pixel ratio capped at 1.5.

### Task 5: Screens

**Files:**
- Create: `src/js/walk/screens.js`
- Modify: `src/css/walk.css`

`CSS3DRenderer` over the WebGL canvas. Each stop's `<article>` becomes a
`CSS3DObject` sized to its device (newspaper, laptop, phone), placed at the
figure's hands, facing back over the shoulder. Screens are sharp and lit:
paper is off-white with serif type, laptop and phone are dark glass with
light UI. Opacity follows `focus` so they surface as the camera turns.

### Task 6: Camera and input

**Files:**
- Modify: `src/js/walk/main.js`

A tall scroll spacer drives `progress`; camera position is the path at `t`,
looking ahead along the path and turning toward the stop's screen by `focus`;
smoothed per frame; faint handheld sway from layered sines. Arrow keys and
space jump to the next or previous stop (`progressOf`). Resize handled.
Reduced motion: no canvas, the plain articles stay.
