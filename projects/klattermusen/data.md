      [Klättermusens Verkstad](https://www.instagram.com/klattermusens.verkstad/)
      is the workshop and store of Swedish outdoor brand
      [Klättermusen](https://www.klattermusen.com/), a short walk from my studio.
      They wanted their mark tufted at two by two metres for the wall of the space.


      Rather than send a flat mockup, I built them a
      [previewer](https://klattermusen.jonasjohansson.se/). It maps artwork onto
      the tufting grid in real yarn colours — the ranges carried by suppliers like
      [Hitex](https://www.hitex.se/) — simulates how the cut pile will render it,
      keeps a running estimate
      of material cost, and drops the result onto a photograph of the wall it was
      headed for. The palette came out of that back-and-forth: Salmon, Ochre
      Yellow, Aubergine Purple, Maple Brown, and a Light Pink carrying the line
      work.


      We made it on their shop floor over the first week of August with
      [Rose Hallgren](https://rosehallgren.se/), on the modular frame from
      [Tufting Ex Machina](/tufting-ex-machina/). The design was projected onto
      the stretched cloth and traced by hand — in mirror, since a tufting gun
      punches from the back and every letter has to be drawn in reverse. Then
      14 mm of cut pile, glued, trimmed, and backed.


      It came off the frame at around twenty kilos, which made hanging a design
      problem of its own. A curtain rod would have stood it too far off the wall,
      so Rose sewed pockets along the back for a 50 mm aluminium flat bar, and I
      printed brackets for it to sit in. They came off the printer twice — the
      first mount hung very slightly askew, so the second set carries an offset
      to correct it.
