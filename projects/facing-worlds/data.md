  - type: text
    content: >-
      Unreal Tournament’s most famous map, rebuilt in the browser as a
      five-a-side capture-the-flag game anyone can join.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: >-
      It began as a sheet of AR stickers I brought to Unreal Fest in Stockholm
      in September 2025. I was working on light and installation for the Epic
      Games event, produced by [Happy Times](https://happytimes.se), and made
      this outside the brief. Pointing a phone at a sticker placed the whole
      arena on the table: a small portal back to a game I had loved since I
      was a teenager.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: >-
      Since then it has grown into a game. Open
      [facingworlds.org](https://facingworlds.org) on a computer and you drop
      straight into the match. A server fills two five-player teams with bots,
      makes room as people join, and keeps the shots, damage, deaths, flags and
      score in sync, with six weapons, pickups, the UT99 HUD, the original
      sounds and announcer, and a live scoreboard.
  - type: image
    src: current-gameplay.jpg
    alt: "A first-person player aims an Enforcer across the bridge at the red tower while the UT99-style HUD shows health, ammunition and both flags."
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: >-
      The firing cadence, weapon poses, projectiles, muzzle flashes and impact
      effects follow the original UnrealScript wherever the browser can. It is
      still deliberately rough and immediate: click the page, grab a flag and
      run.
  - type: text
    content: >-
      On a phone, the same live match becomes a miniature arena you can walk
      around on the table.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: >-
      Point the camera at the printed marker and the arena holds still on it,
      with the live match playing out inside: players running, carrying flags,
      firing and falling where they are in the game. You can move around the
      table, crouch beside a tower and follow the score and both teams.

  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: >-
      The level is placed from the original map data rather than eyeballed from
      screenshots: two flag bases, twenty player starts, 56 pickups, 166
      navigation points and 592 path connections. I started from a 3D model of
      the map by Harry Clarke, which had shrunk the arena to 43% of its real
      size. Restoring the 71-metre towers brought back the long, exposed
      crossings that give Facing Worlds its rhythm.
