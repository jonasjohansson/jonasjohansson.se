      Emerging Sensation took over
      [Reaktorhallen R1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R1_(nuclear_reactor)),
      the old reactor hall underneath
      [KTH](https://www.kth.se/) in Stockholm, for several weeks in January
      2019. [Malin Bobeck Tadaa](https://malintadaa.com/) initiated the
      project and brought me in alongside [Really Interactive](https://www.really-interactive.se/) game studio.
      Malin had perfected a technique using optic fibre and custom 3D-printed
      parts to weave LED strips directly into her textile sculptures.
      Conductive thread woven into the fabric gave the sculptures capacitive
      touch, so when visitors touched them, the light animations shifted and
      a signal was sent to the
      [HoloLens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_HoloLens) AR layer.

      I built the light animations on [Arduino](https://www.arduino.cc/) and
      a node-based [OSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Sound_Control)
      system that bridged the physical sculptures and the HoloLens. Björn
      Albihn and Jonatan Crafoord at Really Interactive built the mixed
      reality side. I spent days and nights in that reactor hall, listening
      to System of a Down and going slightly insane.
