      Lars Lerin's paintings, voice and archives surround the audience in
      [Det Mörka Ljuset](https://detmorkaljuset.se/), a film by Sara Broos
      made for the walls and floor of [Elverket](https://elverket.com/)
      in Stockholm.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: >-
      My work with Elverket spans the film and the tools we use to make it.
      I'm the technical producer and part of the animation team. Alongside
      the moving images, I built room previews, mapping tools and a system
      for collecting and checking the team's deliveries.


      The film follows Lars from his childhood in Värmland to Lofoten and
      further travels, through his paintings, home movies and own telling
      of his life. It is made for a room roughly 34 metres long and eight
      metres high, with images on all four walls and under your feet.

      I built a 3D preview of the space so we could try images in the room
      while working elsewhere. We can walk through it, look from a seated
      position or use a headset. The doors and exit signs are in there too.
      They matter when a face, a subtitle or the edge of a painting lands
      across an opening.


      The browser version lets us drop images and films onto individual
      surfaces or see the whole composition at once. I also built a
      desktop player for the larger video files, keeping the walls and
      floor in sync. The room studies below come from these previews.

      For Lofoten I worked with groups of paintings spread around the
      room, from a dense arrangement of small images to larger houses,
      mountains and stretches of water. The floor has its own composition,
      continuing the colours and reflections below the walls.


      I developed transitions using wet edges, spreading pigment, light
      and mist, as well as studies with point clouds and flocks of birds.
      The short recording shows a Lofoten animation test playing inside
      the model. The flat layout shows how the wall and floor images fit
      together before projection.

      There is a separate file for each wall and for the floor, across
      all the film's chapters. I made templates and mapping tools to keep
      those files aligned, and a delivery tool where the team could send
      large masters, keep track of versions and check their dimensions
      and timing before playback.


      My work also includes the presentation in Mellanrummet, the smaller
      room beside the hall with its fourteen-metre projection wall, and
      the [website for the film](https://detmorkaljuset.se/).
